Part 1: The Visual Structure of the Sigils

A sigil is like a signature or a phone number; it is a focal point that connects your consciousness to the specific energy stream of the entity.

Since I cannot generate images, I will describe their structures so you can visualize or draw them. You can find visual examples easily online by searching their names followed by "sigil," but these descriptions will help you understand what you are looking at.

1. The Sigil of King Paimon (from the Ars Goetia)

Paimon’s sigil is ancient, complex, and looks like a schematized standing figure representing royalty and antenna-like receptivity.

The Central Axis: The main body of the sigil is a strong vertical line.

The "Head": At the top of the vertical line is a loop or a circle. Emerging from the top of this circle are usually two or three curved prongs, resembling horns, a crown, or antennae reaching upwards for knowledge.

The "Arms": About a third of the way down the central line, a horizontal bar crosses it. At both ends of this horizontal bar, the line curls back onto itself to form distinct loops. These represent dominion and the ability to grasp concepts.

The Base: At the bottom, the vertical line often splits into two "legs" that are grounded, sometimes with small cross-bars or circles at the "feet," symbolizing his stable command over earthly affairs and the cardinal direction of Northwest.

Visual Feeling: It looks regal, balanced, and receptive. It resembles a stylized stick-figure king with complex headgear, standing firmly.

2. The Sigil of Mammon (Modern Demonolatry/Dukante Tradition)

Unlike Paimon, Mammon does not have a sigil in the ancient Ars Goetia. His most common sigil used today is modern, often attributed to the Dukante hierarchy. It is fluid, representing flow and currency.

The Stylized "M": The entire structure vaguely resembles a very ornate capital letter "M".

The Central Dip: The center of the sigil dips down deep in a 'V' shape, then curves upwards.

The Outer Loops: The two outer "legs" of the "M" do not just go straight down; they curl outwards into large, sweeping loops.

The Flow: The lines are usually drawn with a cursive, flowing feel, rather than rigid geometric angles. Sometimes small circles are integrated into the loops, representing coins or points of exchange.

Visual Feeling: It looks like flowing liquid gold, a fancy monogram, or a pathway with loops that brings energy back to the center. It represents circulation and movement.
